# Audit of the four unverified frameworks on the bench

Evidence auditor, 2026-09-22. Register: `research/REPORT.md`, 64 findings (F1 to F64). Finding numbers below refer to that file. Grades use the auditor's scale: strong, moderate, weak, lore, contested. "Ungraded background" marks anything I know from outside the register; it is not evidence here and I say what would raise the grade.

## Summary

None of the four founding frameworks was verified by the seven research runs, and the register's coverage section says so. That is absence of checking, not a negative result. Graded on the register alone: Barkley's executive-function model is **weak** (two of its component claims have pooled-analysis support in child-weighted samples, the intervention claim is untested); calm technology is **lore** (its nearest literature, ambient displays, is design theory with no outcome evidence, F36); Endsley's situation-awareness model is **lore** (imported expertise, no verified claim touches it); and kanban work-in-progress limits are **lore** (nothing checked, and the choice research removes the usual justification for a fixed cap, F27). The good news is that most of what the four agents actually ask the dashboard to do survives on other routes: the trial-tested planning programs for adult ADHD (F23, F24), the interruption studies (F1, F7, F8, F11), the clutter and crowding studies (F31, F35), and the developer resumption studies (F49, F50, F51). The problems are specific sentences that overreach: a "well-evidenced" label on externalised time and implementation intentions, elapsed-day displays on the user's own work, a "single most protective rule" for a cap no study supports, a one-minute re-entry target that inverts its own source, and "flow metrics" for a person the register says is wary of being measured. Each agent's Evidence Notes already concede most of this; the Core Principles above them have not been changed to match, so a reader who stops at the principles gets the unaudited version.

## 1. nd-executive-function-coach (Barkley)

**Framework grade: weak.** Barkley's model has two components in the register with direct if indirect-population support: timing deficits in ADHD (F25, medium, pooled analysis of 55 studies, child-weighted, intervals of seconds) and preference for immediate over delayed reward (F26, medium, 37 comparisons, mostly children and adolescents). The model's central intervention claim, that support must sit at the point of performance and that skills training without environmental restructuring fails, was never verified. The register's best evidence for adult ADHD is in fact a skills-training package (F23, high, two randomized trials against active comparisons), which sits awkwardly with the persona's line that such training "fails" without restructuring. The programs do include externalising tools (a calendar and a list), so the two are not in flat contradiction, but the persona states as settled something the trials did not test. Ungraded background: Barkley's 1997 model and his adult time-reproduction work exist as published sources; verifying them, and any adult-sample delay-discounting or time-estimation study, would raise the grade to moderate.

## 2. nd-calm-technology-designer (Weiser and Brown, Case)

**Framework grade: lore.** Calm technology is a design stance from a practitioner book and two Xerox PARC essays. The register's closest literature is the ambient-display work (F36, high confidence that it is theory only): "Nothing checked shows that a peripheral status display beats notifications." No outcome study of a calm or ambient display reached verification. The stance is nonetheless mostly compatible with what was checked, and four of its seven principles survive at moderate or better through the accessibility standards (F1, F2) and the autistic clutter and ADHD crowding studies (F31, F35). One population warning applies to the whole framework: it was written for neurotypical users, and the ADHD sensory profile includes low registration and seeking as well as sensitivity (F12, medium, 23 of 30 studies in children). A signal designed to "communicate without speaking" may never register. What would raise the grade: any controlled study of a peripheral or glanceable status display with a measured outcome, ideally in adults with ADHD or autism.

## 3. nd-agent-supervision-architect (Endsley)

**Framework grade: lore.** The situation-awareness model, with its three levels and its claim that most supervisory-control errors are level 1 and 2 failures, is imported expertise: no verified claim in the register covers it. Ungraded background: it is a widely cited human-factors model with a measurement instrument validated in aviation and air-traffic control. Even verified, the transfer from trained pilots and controllers to one engineer with ADHD and autism supervising language-model coding agents is a large population and domain jump and would cost a grade. What the register does hold is a small set of studies on developers overseeing agents (F13, medium; F14, medium, one preprint of 12 people) and on developers resuming their own work (F49, high; F50, medium; F51, medium). Those support most of the concrete moves, and F14 delivers a caution the framework does not anticipate. The methodology section's Claude Code facts are verified on the user's machine and are not research claims; they are out of scope here. What would raise the grade: verifying Endsley's 1995 model paper and the 1996 error taxonomy, plus any situation-awareness or alarm-management study in software operations or in supervisory control of automation by non-expert operators.

## 4. nd-flow-and-priority-specialist (DeGrandis)

**Framework grade: lore.** Work-in-progress limits, personal kanban, and the five-thieves taxonomy produced no verified claim across seven runs; the coverage section lists them as never checked, alongside Eisenhower, MoSCoW, and Getting Things Done. Ungraded background: WIP limits descend from Lean manufacturing and Little's law, which is arithmetic about queues, not evidence that a person with scarce executive function does better under a cap. The register does support a soft version of the method: few projects per day beats many with no threshold (F52, medium, mined GitHub data, code volume as output), self-interruption costs more than developers believe (F53, one study), a day feels productive through progress on planned goals (F54), and one calendar plus one task list as the first move (F23, F24, high). What the register withholds is any basis for a fixed number (F27, and the report's priorities recs 7 and 8) and any evidence that a cap, rather than a planning moment, is the protective ingredient. What would raise the grade: any controlled or field study of WIP limits or task-count limits for individual knowledge workers, ideally with ADHD.

## 5. Shared gaps worth a targeted research run

All four agents rely on at least one of the following, and the register does not cover it.

1. **Timers, countdowns, and elapsed-time displays in adults with ADHD.** The coach's time principle, the architect's projections, and the flow specialist's age inputs all assume they help. F25 says nothing checked shows it. Highest priority; it touches three agents.
2. **Work-in-progress or task-count limits for individuals.** The flow specialist's whole mechanism, the coach's "few things at once", and the architect's supervised-session cap. Nothing checked. F52 is the only neighbour and has no threshold.
3. **Implementation intentions in adults with ADHD.** The coach encodes them; F21 is children on one lab task. Ungraded background says adult meta-analyses exist in general populations.
4. **Breaks, hyperfocus, and exit prompts.** The coach's hyperfocus principle and the architect's idle thresholds. Never checked, and F17 suggests prompts may not work for autistic inertia.
5. **Supervisory control of multiple autonomous agents by one person, and alarm management.** The architect's framework. F13 and F14 are the only data; both small, one a preprint, neither about many long parallel sessions.
6. **Peripheral and glanceable displays with measured outcomes, and cue salience under low registration.** The designer's founding principle. F36 confirms the literature is theory; F12 warns subtle cues may not register.
7. **Progressive disclosure versus memory load.** The designer hides detail behind a step; F6 says nothing should depend on remembering. No study reconciles them.
8. **Transparency and trust calibration for agent output.** The architect's transparency principle rests on one preprint (F14) that found the opposite of what the principle assumes.
9. **Demand avoidance in response to limits and prompts.** The flow specialist's notes raise it via F17 and F22; no source on demand avoidance was checked.
10. **Any of the above in adults who are autistic and ADHD, or twice-exceptional.** The register's standing limit. Every finding cited here was downgraded at least once for population transfer.
