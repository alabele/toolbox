# 016 — Slack as a fourth source

**Asked:** "I need help organizing my slack." Two days later, after a channel triage and a measurement pass: 119 people asked her something in 90 days and she answered 38, almost all within twelve minutes. She does not miss things because nobody told her fast enough. She misses things that never enter her field of view.

**Roundtable:** interaction designer, art director, flow and priority, attention and interruption, lived-experience advocate. The full problem statement is in `SLACK.md`; this file holds only what changed a commitment.

## Decided

- **The promise is "nothing falls out," not "tell me sooner."** Every ask gets a row, and a row leaves only when she answered it, decided not to, or handed it off. Delivery timing sits on top of that; it is not the thing.
- **Sort by what an item wants, not how it arrived.** DM, mention and channel are Slack's categories and they predict her workload badly — two of thirteen live items tagged her and wanted nothing, two never tagged her and were hers. The words do not appear in the interface.
- **Three dispositions:** Now, Wants an answer, Nothing to do. Acuity, as in triage: what breaks if she reaches it tomorrow, never who shouted.
- **Facts promote an emergency; the model only vetoes.** A person she named, a word she named, a channel she named. At one real emergency every 45 days, a model asked "is this urgent?" cries wolf weekly and she stops trusting it inside a month.
- **The hour is a floor, not a trigger.** A nudge fires when something is waiting, never more than once an hour, and empty hours stay silent. Nine working hours against 3.2 asks a day means a fixed hourly nudge would mostly find nothing.
- **No indicators.** No desktop notification, no dock badge, no unread bold, no count of things undone on any default screen. The emergency path bypasses the nudge; nothing else does.
- **Nothing moves while she reads.** The poll runs every two minutes and queues behind the stale marker from 006.
- **Seen is the app's own mark, and unseen decays.** A user token cannot read Slack's read state, so reading in Slack will not clear a row. Expiry, not clicking, is the exit: opening marks seen, and anything with nothing to do ages out by itself.
- **An ask is a workflow, so a row opens into an intake:** what was asked, what the harness found with a file and line for every claim, and drafted proposals — a ticket, an answer, a reply. One review, a separate approval per proposal.
- **The harness works ahead up to anything other people would see.** Reading a repository, reading Jira, running a session and writing a draft all happen before she arrives. Filing a ticket and sending a message wait for her.
- **The measure is steps, not minutes.** The intake earns its place by turning four steps into one, and it earns it even when the wall clock does not move. A four-step list is not four times one step; it is the thing that does not get started.
- **No Slack visual idiom.** No avatars, initial bubbles, unread bold, reaction chips, presence dots or channel pills. One plate per conversation, speaker names in a fixed left column, each message a line, so repeats stack visibly and it reads as a playscript.
- **Quoted text is its own block.** A Slack message has two layers — what the person typed, and what they pasted or quoted. Flattening them reads as one run-on sentence and hides who she is answering.

## Amended

The roundtable was told that messages in the 23 channels she reads in full are hers to read and not to answer, and all five vetoed them as rows on that basis. The brief was wrong: those are the channels where she is the SME, and the measured zeros sit in exactly those channels. The veto does not survive the correction, which makes the triage load-bearing rather than a convenience.

## Not yet

- What "acceptably responsive" means per relationship. A partner channel and an internal one do not carry the same expectation, and no amount of history can derive it.
- What happens to an ask she never answers — picked up, asked again, or stalled. It decides how hard a row pushes.
- Where the register lives. Four layouts were drawn; she rejected the one that ranks Slack beside her own work and leans toward the one split by what each item wants.
- What a nudge looks like mid-session. The attention researcher allows a line at a boundary she already crosses and vetoes one that fires because time passed; the hourly floor is a clock, so the two need reconciling.
